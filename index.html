Abstract

The rapid evolution of AI agents’ ability to imitate human behavior causes traditional CAPTCHA (Completely Automated Public Turing Test to Tell Computers and Humans Apart) to become increasingly obsolete. Cognitive science has sought to identify cognitive gaps between humans and artificial agents to develop a new generation of CAPTCHA systems. In contrast, posthumanism suggests that the integration of humans and artificial intelligence is inevitable, thereby suggesting that CAPTCHA obsolete in the long run. In this paper, I will argue that any approaches to CAPTCHA development that exploit cognitive differences (e.g., IQ-style tests) will not be plausible in the long run. I reckon that the problem lies in the nature of the Turing Test that was created to convert the ontological question ‘whether a machine can think’ into an epistemological question ‘whether people can differentiate human and machine in the imitation game’. This epistemological question is later misused as an ontological answer to the question ‘Are you a robot?’

In March 2026, a group of software engineers led by Jia Cheng Liu published a project to replace traditional CAPTCHA with a more advanced version. NextGen reCAPTCHA is expected to replace traditional CAPTCHA due to agents’ ability to bypass it. NextGen CAPTCHA is developed by leveraging differences in the cognitive abilities of humans and AI agents, including 4D recognition, scene structure, temporality, numerosity, state tracking, and action (https://503.wiki). The tests or puzzles are slightly more difficult than the traditional version, but they can eliminate more than 90% of bots. The problem is that AI cognitive ability develops at a much faster pace, and the Turing Test was never meant to solve ontological problems in the first place.

Jiacheng outlines a pattern where, every time CAPTCHA moved into new territory, AI caught up; the so-called arms race means every defence eventually got breached:

    • Perception era: In the early CAPTCHA years, CAPTCHA relied on distorting the text in a way it cannot be readable by bots, only by humans. Unfortunately, these systems were defeated by CNN as they achieved superhuman accuracy in OCR (Optical Character Recognition). When image-based tasks were used to replace distorted text, they did not hold for long, as nowadays, visual models like Vision Transformers can accurately predict and interpret visual scenes reliably, thus making current visual CAPTCHA obsolete.
      Reasoning era: "logic CAPTCHA" puzzles require spatial reasoning, game-like thinking, but multimodal large language models are now capable of understanding instructions and can even solve these reasoning CAPTCHA's with high accuracy rates, more than the security threshold.
      Agentic era (Now): New agents do not merely solve CAPTCHA. They navigate between interfaces and execute extensive human-like actions. CAPTCHA cannot differentiate between humans and such agents.

    • At the end of the project, Jiacheng Liu and the team recognized some risks, misuse, fairness, accessibility, and user burden, “CAPTCHAs can impose friction and may disproportionately impact users with disabilities or limited access to audio/visual modalities.” (Jiacheng, 2026, p.9) In the Impact Statement of Next-Gen CAPTCHA, Jiacheng stated that the aim is to support the advancement and safe deployment of machine learning by identifying specific weaknesses in existing agents and offering a scalable, user-friendly defense against automated misuse. The main advantage of this work is the potential to strengthen web security and reliability. More robust, agent-resistant CAPTCHA can reduce issues such as automated fraud, credential stuffing, spam, large-scale account creation, and data scraping. In addition, the proposed benchmark and platform can contribute to research on safer and more dependable interactive agents by enabling consistent evaluation of perception–action integration, memory, and manipulation in real-world scenarios. However, as with many security-related publications, Next-Gen CAPTCHA could be dual-use. Detailed descriptions of CAPTCHA design and solver behavior may aid adversaries in developing more effective bypass strategies or in stress-testing deployed defenses. To reduce this risk, they focused on principles for agent-defensive challenge design and on evaluation methodology. On top of that, they also focused on procedural generation, which enables rapid instance diversification, which can reduce the value of static memorization-based attacks.

    Key Issues

    As internet usage increases worldwide, online platforms are increasingly exposed to automated programs, malicious bots, spam, and even more serious attacks. Web applications provide things such as user registrations, user polls/surveys, public services, and user communication; these websites are the most susceptible to misuse. Many businesses adopt reCAPTCHA, an anti-bot technology, to address these problems. According to Luis von Ahn, there are 5 important areas that require the distinction between robot and human:


    CAPTCHAs is neccesary in several real-world security problems where distinguishing humans from automated programs (bots) is crucial.

    📊
    1: Online Polls
    Online polls gather vast amounts of public opinion and, due to their ease of access, can be vulnerable to bot manipulation. Unprotected polls can be stormed by automated scripts, drowning them in fake votes, resulting in skewed statistics and causing confusion for organizations relying on the data for decisions. ReCAPTCHA ensures that voters are actual people and preserves the veracity and fairness of the results. Traditional safeguards, such as protecting polls with IP restrictions or cookies, are not enough anymore as bot technology quickly adapts.
    📧
    2: Free Email Services
    Free email services are prime targets for cybercriminals, which is why security measures such as reCAPTCHA are used more and more each day. Its most important function is to block bots from mass-creating accounts. A mass-created account, operated by bots, can be used for spam campaigns, phishing attacks, malware distribution, and hacking by allowing them to guess email addresses from these newly created fake accounts, thereby giving hackers access to the online world. Without reCAPTCHA, bots can register thousands of bot email accounts in minutes because there are no security checks.
    🔎
    3: Search Engine Bots
    Some websites wish to prevent indexing by search engines. While HTML tags exist to request bots to stay away, they offer no guarantee of fully repelling the web crawlers.
    🕶
    4: Worms and Spam
    Worms and Spam can be stated as some of the most consistent cybersecurity threats because of the way these make use of automation to rapidly spread throughout networks and across online infrastructures. Spam bots bombard inboxes, comment sections, and forms with unsolicited content and sometimes malicious content, whereas worms use accessible online features as an entryway for them to self-replicate and spread without any human interference. However, reCAPTCHA tries to fight all of these threats through human verification, which will be harder for the automated systems to bypass. Since current bots send out messages in bursts of millions of messages every day, it has a lot of effect and can be prevented to an extent with some efforts (such as banning IP addresses for bots, etc. ). ReCAPTCHA makes use of detection based on the behaviour of the networks for security, which ensures that both malware can be monitored for detection. However, banning IPs or using detection methods on such a huge volume of traffic every day can cause problems.
    🔒
    5: Preventing Dictionary Attacks
    Captcha is important to prevent dictionary attacks in password systems. The idea is to prevent a computer from being able to iterate through the entire space of passwords by requiring a human to type the passwords. Without CAPTCHA, a bot can keep trying to guess passwords in the login form and will eventually crack the password.


    Posthumanist View

    “What is the posthuman? Think of it as a point of view characterized by the following assumptions. (I do not mean this list to be exclusive or definitive. Rather, it names elements found at various sites. It is meant to be suggestive rather than prescriptive.) First, the posthuman view privileges informational pattern over material instantiation, so that embodiment in a biological substrate is seen as an accident of history rather than an inevitability of life. Second, the posthuman view considers consciousness, regarded as the seat of human identity in the Western tradition long before Descartes thought he was a mind thinking, as an epiphenomenon, as an evolutionary upstart trying to claim that it is the whole show when, in actuality it is only a minor sideshow. Third, the posthuman view thinks of the body as the original prosthesis we all learn to manipulate, so that extending or replacing the body with other prostheses becomes a continuation of a process that began before we were born. Fourth, and most important, by these and other means, the posthuman view configures human being so that it can be seamlessly articulated with intelligent machines. In the posthuman, there are no essential differences or absolute demarcations between bodily existence and computer simulation, cybernetic mechanism and biological organism, robot teleology and human goals.” (Hayles, 1999, pp.2-3)

    Option 1: Next-Gen Captcha

    Next-Gen CAPTCHAs is being built with a scalable defense framework with 27 novel CAPTCHA families1 designed to secure the next-generation web against advanced agents. The engineers exploit the persistent human-agent "Cognitive Gap" in interactive perception, memory, decision-making, and action. By engineering dynamic tasks that require adaptive intuition rather than granular planning, we re-establish a robust distinction between biological users and artificial agents. The new captcha designs are based on cognitive gaps which can be categorized as:


    G1

    Scene-Structure Inference

    Observation interpretation and grounding under partial observability

    Mirror, Shadow Direction, 3D Viewpoint, Backmost Layer
    G2

    Temporal Integration

    Multi-step evidence accumulation from motion and sequential reveals

    Spooky Circle, Structure From Motion, Trajectory Recovery
    G3

    Numerosity & Invariants

    Decision-boundary sensitivity to discrete quantities and counts

    Hole Counting, Color Counting, Subway Paths
    G4

    Latent-State Tracking

    Working-memory consistency across interaction steps

    Dice Roll Path, Box Folding, Temporal Object Continuity
    G5

    Perception-to-Action

    Robust low-level execution of correct browser interactions

    Static Jigsaw, Dynamic Jigsaw, Red Dot
